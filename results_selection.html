Results: Selection Pressure & Site Conservation

To design primers that remain effective over time, we must understand the evolutionary forces acting on the viral genome. Module 05 executes the HyPhy Selection Pressure Engine (Pond et al. 2020) to analyze codon-specific selection using two complementary algorithms:

  1. FUBAR (Fast Unconstrained Bayesian Approximation) (Murrell et al. 2013): Identifies sites under pervasive negative (purifying) selection (\(\text{dN} < \text{dS}\)). These sites are highly conserved and represent the most stable targets for primer design.
  2. MEME (Mixed Effects Model of Episodic Selection) (Murrell et al. 2012): Identifies sites under episodic diversifying selection (\(\text{dN} > \text{dS}\) at specific tree branches). These sites are actively evolving and must be excluded from critical primer regions (e.g., the 3’ terminal block).

1. Pervasive Purifying Selection (FUBAR Analysis)

FUBAR was run on the Matrix Protein 1 (M1, 253 codons) and Matrix Protein 2 (M2, 98 codons) coding sequences. The vast majority of codons in M1 exhibit strong negative selection, reflecting the structural constraints of the protein shell:

  • M1 Selection Profile
  • M2 Selection Profile
  • Matrix Protein 1 (M1) Conservation:
    • Out of 253 codons, 192 sites (75.9%) were identified under strong pervasive purifying selection with a posterior probability \(\ge 0.90\).
    • This extreme conservation explains why the regional designs (Indo-only and SEA-only) were able to place highly sensitive primers in the forward block (codons 63 to 117, corresponding to nucleotide positions 187–352 bp).
  • Matrix Protein 2 (M2) Conservation:
    • Out of 98 codons, only 25 sites (25.5%) were identified under purifying selection. M2 exhibits higher sequence entropy than M1, particularly in its extracellular domain (ectodomain), making it less suitable for diagnostic design.

2. Episodic Diversifying Selection (MEME Analysis)

MEME was run to detect transient, branch-specific evolutionary events that standard site-level models miss:

  • Identified Episodic Sites in M1:
    • Only 2 codons (Codons 30 and 235) exhibited evidence of episodic positive selection with a p-value \(\le 0.05\).
    • Diagnostic Veto: Codon 30 is located upstream of the primary forward primer target (which starts at nucleotide position 187). The Bridge Scoring Engine (Module 07) successfully detected this selection marker and applied a spatial penalty, ensuring that primer annealing coordinates remain downstream of codon 30.
  • Identified Episodic Sites in M2:
    • 6 codons (Codons 11, 13, 18, 36, 59, and 93) exhibited episodic selection. The clustering of these positive selection sites in the M2 segment highlights the risks of placing diagnostic primers in this gene region.

3. M1 vs. M2 Selection Pressure Divergence

The stark contrast in selection pressure between the two matrix proteins highlights their differing biological constraints:

  • Structural Constraint (M1): The M1 protein is a structural matrix shell that must interact closely with the viral envelope, the ribonucleoprotein (RNP) complexes, and the cytoplasmic tail of the M2 protein. This complex web of physical interactions severely constrains the protein’s sequence space, meaning almost any amino acid change impairs viral assembly and budding. This translates to the 75.9% pervasive purifying selection rate observed, consistent with the high sequence conservation of the M1 gene documented in global surveillance data (Bhatt et al. 2011).
  • Immune & Drug Selection (M2): The M2 protein is a proton-selective ion channel essential for viral uncoating (Pinto and Lamb 2006). Its extracellular ectodomain is exposed to host antibodies, driving immune escape selection pressure. Simultaneously, its transmembrane pore domain is under strong drug-mediated selection from adamantane antivirals (amantadine and rimantadine), which directly drove the global fixation of the S31N substitution in the pore region (Hayden 2006; Deyde et al. 2007). Consequently, the purifying selection rate drops to 25.5%, reflecting the high evolutionary plasticity of this gene and rendering it an unreliable target for long-term diagnostic assay stability.

4. Bridge Scoring Profile & Physical Target Selection

The Bridge Scoring Engine (Module 07) integrated site-specific conservation (Shannon Entropy) with HyPhy FUBAR purifying selection probabilities using a weighted core scoring formula:

\[\text{Core Score} = 0.66 \times (1 - \text{Entropy}) + 0.34 \times \text{Prob(dN < dS)}\]

This core score was computed across the entire segment. Because the engine was configured with the full_segment strategy for the Influenza A MP segment, it evaluated the segment from 0 to 1027 bp as a single candidate region (segment-MP_region_1). The resulting overall scores and grades for this target region are:

  • Indonesia Only: Core composite score of 0.4071 (Grade D, mean entropy: 0.2146, conservation: 89.27%).
  • Southeast Asia Only: Core composite score of 0.4098 (Grade D, mean entropy: 0.2025, conservation: 89.87%).
  • All-Partial Database: Core composite score of 0.4022 (Grade D, mean entropy: 0.2359, conservation: 88.20%).

Downstream Primer Coordinate Selection (Module 08 & 09)

Although Module 07 exported the entire segment as a single candidate zone (due to the segment’s small size and overall conservation), the downstream primer design tool (Primer3/PrimerLab in Module 08 and 09) scanned this candidate region to select the most stable physical primer coordinates, demonstrating how local sequence variations alter target selection:

  • Indonesia Only Design: The optimal primers were placed in the M1 gene at 187–341 bp (Forward primer: 187–218 bp, Reverse primer: 310–341 bp), generating a 155 bp amplicon.
  • Southeast Asia Only Design: The primers were placed at 187–352 bp (Forward: 187–216 bp, Reverse: 321–352 bp), generating a 166 bp amplicon.
  • All-Partial Database Design: When global database variations were introduced, increased sequence entropy in the M1 gene (including at codon 30) forced the primer design tool to shift coordinates to the 3’ end of the segment in the M2 gene at 858–1008 bp (Forward: 858–889 bp, Reverse: 978–1008 bp), generating a 151 bp amplicon.
