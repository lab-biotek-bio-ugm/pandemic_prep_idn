Results: Design Database Comparison

The primer/probe sets were designed three times, each against a different sequence database, to test how the choice of design cohort shapes the final assay:

  1. Indonesia Only (indo-only) — local Indonesian strains only.
  2. Southeast Asia (sea-only) — Indonesia + Malaysia + Singapore (the SEA countries actually represented in the MP dataset; Singapore is the largest cohort).
  3. All-Partial (all-partial) — dense focal Indonesian strains plus a sparse global contextual background (every other country).

All three were then evaluated on the same global surveillance set. The headline is counter-intuitive and is the point of this page: the design with the highest raw coverage is not the best assay. indo-only is the practical winner — the only design that is simultaneously high-coverage on both detection lenses, safe against human DNA, and able to produce a multiplex assay that passes the non-negotiable safety gates.

Note

Figures on this page are transcribed from the July 2026 runs of the three builds (Flu A MP + Flu B MP). They will become render-time computed values once the results pages are made data-driven (see docs/development/report-data-driven-plan.md).

1. Overall In-Silico Coverage (global set)

Rank-1 candidate of each build, measured on the global surveillance set (Flu A: \(N=50{,}750\); Flu B: \(N=7{,}248\)):

Target Design DB PCR amplified % RAA detected %
Flu A MP indo-only 91.47 93.14
sea-only 95.68 62.07
all-partial 93.88 95.12
Flu B MP indo-only 99.68 99.81
sea-only 99.68 99.81
all-partial 99.68 99.81

All three builds use the current decoupled M10, so the PCR and RAA columns are directly comparable.

ImportantRead the numbers carefully

Flu B is essentially maxed for every design (99.68% / 99.81%). For Flu A, the highest PCR (sea-only, 95.68%) and the highest RAA (all-partial, 95.12%) each belong to a design that fails elsewhere — sea-only’s RAA assay is globally blind to H3N2 (§2) and all-partial’s primers amplify human DNA (§3). indo-only (91.47% / 93.14%) is the only design that is competitive on both lenses and survives the safety gates.

2. The sea-only RAA Blind Spot (probe vs global H3N2)

sea-only Flu A shows PCR 95.68% but RAA-detected only 62.07% — a large, biologically backwards gap. Breaking it down by subtype shows the cause is the probe, concentrated in one subtype:

Subtype N PCR amplified % RAA amplified % RAA detected %
A / H3N2 19,258 97.87 97.87 2.66
A / H1N1 18,468 99.15 99.53 99.12
A / H9N2 4,315 77.87 97.40 97.40
A / H5N1 1,784 96.69 99.61 99.61

The sea-only primers amplify H3N2 fine (97.87%), but the probe detects only 2.66% of it. Because H3N2 is the single largest subtype globally (~38% of the set), that one probe failure drags the whole RAA-detected figure to 62%. The design DB (Singapore-heavy) steered the amplicon+probe to a region that is conserved regionally but divergent in global H3N2 — a classic regional design, globally biased outcome (Khan and Cheung 2020). Notably indo-only, designed on Indonesian data alone, happened to pick a better-conserved probe region that works across all major subtypes globally (RAA 91%).

3. Safety: Off-Target Amplicon on Human DNA (the decisive gate)

The selection engine (scripts/tools/candidate_selection.py) applies Class-1 safety gates that are never relaxed, because violating them makes the assay wrong rather than merely worse: an off-target amplicon on human DNA, an off-target against another virus, a human binding site with the 3′ end fully anchored, a dye-channel collision, or a cross-dimer past the veto. A last-resort selection level (L5) waives every quality floor (coverage, grade, WARNING) — but it keeps these Class-1 gates.

human_products counts predicted amplicons on the human genome (both primers producing a product) — a direct false-positive mechanism. For the top-10 Flu A candidates of each build:

Design (Flu A) top-10 with a human amplicon human-safe candidates (all ranks) first safe rank rank-1 human grade
indo-only 0 / 10 75 / 78 1 A
sea-only 0 / 10 47 / 98 1 A
all-partial 10 / 10 22 / 98 11 C

Every one of all-partial’s top-10 Flu A candidates can amplify human DNA. Its rank-1 worst hit binds Homo sapiens T2T-CHM13 chromosome 4 with the 3′ end fully anchored (0 overhang), 1 mismatch, 68.8% of the primer bound. Since any patient sample contains human DNA, such an assay would false-positive — so the gate correctly refuses it, and this is exactly why all-partial returns no recommended multiplex (§4).

4. Multiplex Outcome per Scenario

Pairing each build’s Flu A and Flu B candidates (FAM × HEX) and applying the selection ladder:

Design pairs screened passed (level) Rank-1 recommended pair
indo-only 15 6 (L1) FluA_Rank10_x_FluB_Rank12
sea-only 90 14 (L1) FluA_Rank3_x_FluB_Rank8
all-partial 0 0 None — no combination passed

all-partial returns “No Recommended Combination” not because the ladder failed to relax, but because every top Flu A candidate trips the Class-1 human-amplicon gate (§3) — an outcome the pipeline reports rather than hides.

5. Singleplex View & the Database-Scope Divergence

Dropping multiplex does not rescue all-partial: the human-amplicon problem is a property of the Flu A primers themselves, not of pairing. Per-target, rank-1 candidate:

Target Design rank-1 human-safe grade PCR % RAA det %
Flu A indo-only yes A 91.47 93.14
sea-only yes A 95.68 62.07
all-partial no C 93.88 95.12
Flu B indo-only yes B 99.68 99.81
sea-only yes B 99.68 99.81
all-partial yes B 99.68 99.81
ImportantThe database only matters for Flu A — and only for global H5N1
  • Flu B — the design database makes no difference: all three reach 99.68% / 99.81% and stay human-safe. (The 83% once shown for indo-only Flu B was an old-M10 under-count, now corrected.)
  • Flu A — the databases are close on overall PCR (91–96%); the real divergence is global A/H5N1: indo-only covers only ~32% (its Indonesian clade-2.1.3 design misses the global clade-2.3.4.4b panzootic, even under RAA), while the broader sea/all-partial cohorts catch it — but at the cost of their disqualifying flaws (probe-blind H3N2; human amplicon).

So all-partial’s multiplex failure is entirely the Flu A side. The genuinely open question is global-H5N1 breadth for Flu A; a targeted design that adds recent clade-2.3.4.4b sequences to the indo-only cohort (keeping its safety and H3N2/H1N1 strength) is a plausible future direction, out of scope here.

6. Why the all-partial “Rescue” Is Not Worth It

all-partial Flu A does have 22 human-safe candidates — but the first appears only at rank 11, and they are thermodynamically weak. Lower penalty scores are better:

Flu A candidate set p3_penalty vienna_penalty (secondary structure) final_score
indo-only rank-1 (reference) 8.3 0 6
all-partial top-10 (human-unsafe) 10.7 – 11.0 40 – 44 43 – 52
all-partial human-safe (rank 11+) 10.9 – 12.2 39 – 44 53 – 140

The distinguishing metric is the ViennaRNA secondary-structure penalty: ~42 for every all-partial primer versus 0 for indo-only — the all-partial amplicon region self-folds, which can depress amplification efficiency. The human-safe all-partial candidates are worse-ranked than even its own unsafe top-10. Recovering a safe all-partial Flu A assay therefore means accepting a self-folding primer far below the indo-only design.

7. Conclusion

Design Flu A / Flu B coverage Human-safe (Flu A) Multiplex Verdict
indo-only 91.5% / 99.7% (both lenses) yes 6 pairs pass ✅ recommended
sea-only 95.7% PCR / RAA broken (H3N2) yes 14 pairs pass ⚠️ globally probe-blind
all-partial 93.9% / 99.7% no (top candidates) none ❌ amplifies human DNA

indo-only is the recommended design: human-safe, competitive on both PCR and RAA, structurally clean (ViennaRNA penalty 0), strong on the human-seasonal subtypes and on Flu B (99.7%), and the source of a multiplex assay that passes the non-negotiable safety gates. The sea-only and all-partial builds are valuable as contrast cases — adding regional or global data pushes the universal Flu A design into worse territory (a probe blind spot, or human cross-amplification), so the indo-only choice is robust rather than incidental. Its one documented limitation, reported plainly, is global A/H5N1 (~32%): its Indonesian clade-2.1.3 design covers local H5N1 (98.7%) but not the globally dominant clade 2.3.4.4b — a preparedness/importation gap that would need a targeted design update, not a routine-use hole.
