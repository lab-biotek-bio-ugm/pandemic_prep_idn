Results: In-silico Performance Validation

This section details the final in-silico validation of the designed primer and probe candidates against a global database of 50,750 Influenza A sequences. We evaluate the diagnostic sensitivity of the indo-only design, analyze the impact of degenerate bases on sequence coverage, and provide direct access to the validation heatmaps and report files.

1. Overall In-Silico Coverage and Degeneracy Uplift

To verify the necessity of IUPAC degenerate bases, all candidate sets were evaluated in two forms: 1. Degenerate: Primers and probes containing degenerate bases (e.g., R for A/G, Y for C/T) as designed by the pipeline. 2. Non-Degenerate: Primers and probes where degenerate bases were replaced with their consensus base, simulating non-degenerate diagnostic design.

The table below summarizes the overall PCR and RAA amplification coverage achieved on the global dataset (\(N=50,750\) sequences), demonstrating the degeneracy uplift (gain):

Scenario PCR (Non-Degen) PCR (Degenerate) PCR Uplift RAA (Non-Degen) RAA (Degenerate) RAA Uplift
Indonesia Only
(indo-only)		 18.61% 91.20% +72.59% 18.61% 91.16% +72.55%
ImportantRead RAA vs PCR carefully: the RAA column is currently PCR-capped

For indo-only, degenerate RAA (91.16%) sits just below degenerate PCR (91.20%) overall — and for divergent subtypes the gap is large and backwards (global A/H5N1 RAA reads 31.84%). That ordering is wrong for the chemistry — RAA tolerates more mismatches than PCR — and it is a modelling artifact, not biology: M10 uses the PCR seqkit -m 3 result as the amplification gate and only then applies the RAA tolerance, so the RAA figure is a subset of PCR and cannot exceed it (global A/H5N1 is ~94% RAA-viable, not 31.84%). See the Module 10 page (§1.3) and the coverage-failure attribution (M12; output in results/<build>/m12/ and surfaced in the QC dossier) for the corrected two-lens view. A fix (running seqkit at the RAA mismatch budget) is prepared; the numbers here are what the current pipeline produced and are PCR-conservative for an RAA assay.

Key Findings:

  • The Degeneracy Necessity: For the indo-only design, removing degenerate bases collapses the coverage from 91.20% down to 18.61% (a drop of over 70 percentage points). This demonstrates mathematically that IUPAC degenerate bases are essential to accommodate the natural genomic variation in circulating Influenza A strains (Elnifro et al. 2000). Non-degenerate primers carrying a consensus-only sequence are systematically excluded from large fractions of the viral sequence space, consistent with documented primer mismatch failures in diagnostic assays (Khan and Cheung 2020).

3. Subtype-Specific Performance Analysis

Coverage was analyzed across major Influenza A subtypes. Read the database carefully: the table below is coverage against the design / focal DB (the sequences each design was built on), which is why the numbers are high. It is NOT the global figure — see the global callout beneath it.

Design / focal-DB coverage (RAA detected %; N = design-DB sequences of that subtype):

Subtype Indo-Only (focal)
A / H5N1 98.72% (N=312)
A / H3N2 99.58% (N=238)
A / H1N1 96.25% (N=160)
ImportantThe same subtypes on the GLOBAL surveillance set (indo-only design) tell a different story

Against the 50,750-sequence global set, indo-only per-subtype RAA-detected coverage is H5N1 31.84%, H3N2 ~99.0%, H1N1 ~96.9%. The H5N1 drop is not a design failure on the target population (focal = 98.7%): it is the strict-PCR gate artifact of §1.3 (M10) — under RAA tolerance ~94% of global H5N1 is still detected, and only ~6% (clade 2.3.4.4b / Japan) truly fails. The design covers what circulates in Indonesia; the global H5N1 gap is an importation/preparedness signal, not a routine-use hole.

  • Subtype Inclusivity (focal / target population): the indo-only design is highly effective across all major subtypes it was built on, achieving \(>96\%\) on H5N1, H3N2, and H1N1.

4. Temporal Robustness & Antigenic Drift

Breaking the indo-only degenerate coverage down by collection year (from the M10 breakdowns.year table) shows how the assay tracks viral evolution over the past decade against the global set:

  • Coverage is not flat: it holds in the high-80s to low-90s for most years (2015 87.4%, 2016 89.0%, 2017 88.8%, 2018 88.1%, 2019 93.7%), dips to a low of 74.4% in 2020, and then recovers strongly through the most recent seasons (2023 92.8%, 2024 93.4%, 2025 96.7%).
  • The 2020 trough coincides with the pandemic-era collapse in influenza sampling (only 1,393 sequences that year) and a shift in circulating lineages; the subsequent recovery to >93% indicates the 187–341 bp target region remains well matched to the strains dominating 2023–2025.
  • Because RAA detection is currently PCR-capped (§1), these per-year figures are the PCR-conservative floor; the RAA-viable coverage in the divergent years is higher (see M12).
