Results: In-silico Performance Validation

This section validates the designed primer/probe sets in silico across the three design databases — indo-only, sea-only, and all-partial — evaluated on the same global surveillance set (Flu A MP: \(N=50{,}750\); Flu B MP: \(N=7{,}248\)). indo-only remains the main design thread of this report; the other two are shown for comparison. The full head-to-head trade-off (safety, multiplex, thermodynamics) is on the Design DB Comparison page.

NoteAll three builds use the same (decoupled) M10 model

indo-only was re-run with the current M10, in which RAA is decoupled from the PCR gate, so all three scenarios are now directly comparable on both the PCR and RAA lenses. This re-run also corrected two figures from an earlier draft: indo-only Flu B coverage (an old-M10 build under-counted the degenerate benefit, reporting 83.22% where the correct value is 99.68%), and global A/H5N1 — the earlier seqkit-free M12 step suggested ~94% RAA-viable, but the real decoupled M10 confirms it stays ~32% (a genuine local-vs-global clade gap, §3), not a modelling artifact.

1. Overall In-Silico Coverage and Degeneracy Uplift

IUPAC degenerate bases were verified by evaluating each candidate in two forms — degenerate (as designed) and non-degenerate (degenerate positions collapsed to consensus). Degenerate coverage of the rank-1 candidate on the global set:

Flu A MP (\(N=50{,}750\)):

Design DB PCR non-degen PCR degenerate RAA detected (see caveat)
indo-only 18.64% 91.47% 93.14%
sea-only 80.89% 95.68% 62.07%
all-partial 19.22% 93.88% 95.12%

Flu B MP (\(N=7{,}248\)):

Design DB PCR non-degen PCR degenerate RAA detected
indo-only 83.29% 99.68% 99.81%
sea-only 83.29% 99.68% 99.81%
all-partial 83.29% 99.68% 99.81%

Key findings

  • Degeneracy is essential: collapsing degenerate bases to consensus drops indo-only Flu A PCR coverage from 91.47% to 18.64% (>70 pp), confirming IUPAC bases are needed to absorb natural variation (Elnifro et al. 2000; Khan and Cheung 2020). all-partial shows the same collapse (93.88% → 19.22%); sea-only’s non-degen floor is higher (80.89%) because its consensus already matches a broader regional set.
  • Flu B is essentially maxed for every database: all three reach 99.68% PCR / 99.81% RAA on Flu B — a broader design database gives no Flu B advantage (the earlier 83.22% for indo-only was an old-M10 under-count). The database choice matters for Flu A, and specifically in the per-subtype picture (§3), not in the overall Flu A PCR (91–96% for all three).

3. Subtype-Specific Performance (global set, Flu A)

The clearest view of how the design database matters is the global per-subtype PCR-amplified coverage (fair across scenarios — same seqkit model):

Subtype N indo-only PCR sea-only PCR all-partial PCR
A / H3N2 19,258 99.02% 97.87% 93.81%
A / H1N1 18,468 96.95% 99.15% 97.10%
A / H9N2 4,315 93.81% 77.87% 98.49%
A / H5N1 1,784 31.84% 96.69% 79.54%
  • indo-only is strong on the human-seasonal subtypes it was built around (H3N2 99.0%, H1N1 97.0%) but weak on global A/H5N1 (31.84% PCR; RAA does not rescue it — 31.89%). This is not a modelling artifact and not a strict-PCR quirk: indo-only covers local A/H5N1 at 98.72% (§context below), so the primers work on the H5N1 it was built on. The gap is a clade mismatch — Indonesian H5N1 is clade 2.1.3 (older poultry outbreaks), while global H5N1 is now dominated by clade 2.3.4.4b (the 2021+ panzootic), which differs beyond even the RAA mismatch tolerance. So it is a genuine global-H5N1 / importation gap, not a local-use hole.
  • A broader database buys global-H5N1 breadth: sea-only (96.69%) and all-partial (79.54%) PCR-amplify far more global H5N1 because their design cohorts include the divergent clades — but that advantage is undercut by their disqualifying flaws (sea: probe globally blind to H3N2; all-partial: primers amplify human DNA — see Design DB Comparison).
NoteDesign/focal-DB coverage (context)

On the focal DB it was built on, indo-only reaches \(>96\%\) on every major subtype (A/H5N1 98.72% n=312, A/H3N2 99.58% n=238, A/H1N1 96.25% n=160) — the design covers what circulates locally. The focal-DB per-subtype tables for sea-only/all-partial are dominated by their own (regional / avian-heavy) training composition and are not directly comparable, so the global table above is used for the cross-scenario view.

4. Temporal Robustness & Antigenic Drift (indo-only)

Breaking the indo-only degenerate coverage down by collection year (M10 breakdowns.year) shows how the recommended design tracks viral evolution on the global set:

  • Coverage is not flat: high-80s to low-90s for most years (2015 87.4%, 2016 89.0%, 2017 88.8%, 2018 88.1%, 2019 93.7%), a low of 74.4% in 2020, then recovery to 2023 92.8%, 2024 93.4%, 2025 96.7%.
  • The 2020 trough coincides with the pandemic-era collapse in influenza sampling (1,393 sequences that year) and a lineage shift; recovery to >93% shows the 187–341 bp target stays well matched to 2023–2025 strains.
  • These per-year figures are the decoupled-M10 PCR coverage; the RAA lens tracks it closely for indo-only now that the two are computed separately.
