Results: In-silico Performance Validation

This section details the final in-silico validation of the designed primer and probe candidates against a global database of 50,750 Influenza A sequences. We compare the diagnostic sensitivity of the three design scenarios, analyze the impact of degenerate bases on sequence coverage, and provide direct access to the validation heatmaps and report files.

1. Overall In-Silico Coverage and Degeneracy Uplift

To verify the necessity of IUPAC degenerate bases, all candidate sets were evaluated in two forms: 1. Degenerate: Primers and probes containing degenerate bases (e.g., R for A/G, Y for C/T) as designed by the pipeline. 2. Non-Degenerate: Primers and probes where degenerate bases were replaced with their consensus base, simulating non-degenerate diagnostic design.

The table below summarizes the overall PCR and RAA amplification coverage achieved on the global dataset (\(N=50,750\) sequences), demonstrating the degeneracy uplift (gain):

Key Findings:

  • The Degeneracy Necessity: For the indo-only design, removing degenerate bases collapses the coverage from 91.20% down to 18.61% (a drop of over 70 percentage points). This demonstrates mathematically that IUPAC degenerate bases are essential to accommodate the natural genomic variation in circulating Influenza A strains (Elnifro et al. 2000). Non-degenerate primers carrying a consensus-only sequence are systematically excluded from large fractions of the viral sequence space, consistent with documented primer mismatch failures in diagnostic assays (Khan and Cheung 2020).
  • Regional Design Limitation: The regional sea-only and global all-partial candidates achieved very low global coverage (~34% and ~17% respectively). This is due to sequence entropy at the design target and the fact that their degenerate slots were spent accommodating rare regional sequencing artifacts rather than high-frequency global consensus transitions.

  • Subtype Inclusivity:
    • The indo-only design is highly effective across all major subtypes, achieving \(>96\%\) PCR coverage for H5N1, H3N2, and H1N1.
    • The sea-only design is heavily biased toward H3N2 (\(86.67\%\)), failing to detect H5N1 and H1N1 due to regional sequence mismatches.
    • The all-partial design shows moderate coverage for H5N1 (\(47.37\%\)) and H1N1 (\(35.17\%\)), but fails to detect H3N2 (\(3.17\%\)), indicating a significant design bias.

4. Temporal Robustness & Antigenic Drift

Aggregating simulated detection rates over time shows how well each design resists viral evolution (antigenic drift) from 2015 to 2025:

  • Indo-Only: Maintains stable coverage above 90% across the 10-year span, confirming that the 187–341 bp target region remains highly conserved.
  • SEA-Only: Shows significant annual fluctuations, indicating vulnerability to seasonal mutations.
  • All-Partial: Exhibits a steady decline in coverage in recent years, indicating that its target region is undergoing evolutionary drift in modern global lineages.
