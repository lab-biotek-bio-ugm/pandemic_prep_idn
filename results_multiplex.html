Results: Dual-Target Multiplex Compatibility

To enable simultaneous point-of-care detection of Influenza A and Influenza B in a single reaction tube, candidate assays designed for the Indonesian cohort were evaluated using the Multiplex Compatibility Screening Engine (scripts/m11_multiplex_checker.py).

The screening engine evaluates all pairwise heterodimerization thermodynamics (\(\Delta G\)), kinetic melting temperature alignment (\(\Delta T_m\)), and fluorophore channel separation across a 6-oligo reaction mixture (\(\text{Fwd}_A, \text{Rev}_A, \text{Probe}_A, \text{Fwd}_B, \text{Rev}_B, \text{Probe}_B\)).

1. Dual-Temperature Screening Regimes

To guarantee assay robustness, 100 candidate combinations (\(10 \times 10\) matrix of top Influenza A MP and Influenza B MP candidates) were evaluated across two temperature regimes:

  1. Isothermal RT-RAA Mode (\(39.0^\circ\text{C}\)): Primary operational temperature for point-of-care recombinase-mediated amplification.
  2. qPCR Comparative Benchmark (\(55.0^\circ\text{C}\)): Gold-standard laboratory thermal cycling benchmark.

Key Biophysical Observations:

  • Zero Veto Violations: All top combinations exhibit a worst-case cross-dimer binding energy \(\Delta G > -9.0\text{ kcal/mol}\), preventing spontaneous primer-dimer formation or competition during strand exchange.
  • Kinetic Balance: The rank 1 combination (FluA_Rank7_x_FluB_Rank6) achieves an exceptionally tight \(T_m\) delta of \(0.29^\circ\text{C}\) across all four primers, ensuring synchronized amplification rates.
  • Fluorophore Independence: Influenza A probe uses FAM (\(\lambda_{\text{em}} = 520\text{ nm}\)) and Influenza B probe uses HEX (\(\lambda_{\text{em}} = 556\text{ nm}\)), eliminating optical crosstalk.
