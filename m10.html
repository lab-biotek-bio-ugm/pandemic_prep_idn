Module 10: In-silico PCR & RAA Validation

Before biological wet-lab validation, designed primer and probe candidates must undergo virtual simulation to measure their diagnostic sensitivity. Module 10 executes the In-silico Validation Engine (scripts/m10_insilico_pcr.py) using SeqKit (Shen et al. 2016) to run high-throughput binding simulations against large GISAID and NCBI sequence databases.

This step calculates exact diagnostic coverage metrics broken down by subtype, collection year, and country to verify assay robustness against global and local viral diversity.

1. Virtual Amplification and Binding Simulation

The validation engine runs simulated amplifications by matching designed primers and probes against target database sequences using sequence-matching parameters:

1.1. PCR Simulation (seqkit amplicon)

SeqKit’s amplicon command matches primer sequences against every target genome in the database, allowing up to a defined number of mismatches (default: \(3\) or \(4\) mismatches). It returns virtual amplicons for target genomes that match both primers within a designated length range (e.g. 100–300 bp).

1.2. RAA Isothermal Binding Rules

Because RAA operates at \(39^\circ\text{C}\) with a recombinase complex, it is thermodynamically tolerant of minor internal mismatches but highly sensitive to 3’ terminal defects. The engine implements strict, multi-zone mismatch criteria:

  • 3’ Critical Zone: Evaluates the last 4 nucleotides of the 3’ end (the recombinase “anchor region”) of both primers, allowing a maximum of 1 mismatch; more than 1 vetoes binding. The 4-nt window follows Higgins et al. (2022) — the only study to characterise primer–template mismatches specifically for RPA — which found single mismatches outside the terminal anchor do not significantly affect time-to-positivity. The earlier 8-nt value had no source and under-reported coverage.
  • Internal Body: Allows a maximum of 4 mismatches across the rest of the 30–35 bp primer body.
  • Probe Binding: Probes (46–52 bp) allow a maximum of 5 total mismatches (not 3’-weighted — the probe’s 3’ terminus is C3-blocked and cannot be extended), following the TwistAmp Assay Design Manual §2.7, which reports RPA performance is unaffected by 5–9 mismatches on primers or probe.

1.3. Why the reported RAA coverage is currently PCR-capped

A modelling caveat that materially changes how the numbers below should be read. M10’s authoritative amplification call comes from seqkit amplicon, run at the PCR mismatch budget (max_mismatch = 3). The RAA binding rules in §1.2 are then applied only to sequences seqkit already amplified. Consequently raa_detected_pct is a subset of the PCR-amplified set and can never exceed it — the opposite of RAA biology, in which the recombinase tolerates more mismatches than PCR.

The effect is large for divergent subtypes. Global A/H5N1 clusters at ~5 total primer mismatches — above the PCR -m 3 cut, but within the RAA tolerance (1 in the 3’ anchor, 4 internal). The seqkit gate therefore drops them and the reported figure reads 31.84%, whereas a seqkit-free RAA assessment puts the RAA-viable coverage at ~94%. Only ~6% of global H5N1 fails even the RAA model, concentrated in clade 2.3.4.4b / Japan and driven by a reverse-primer internal substitution. This per-position attribution is produced by the coverage-failure attribution step (M12, scripts/m12_coverage_attribution.py) — a generated artifact under results/<build>/m12/ and surfaced in the per-assay QC dossier, not a separate report page.

The figures on this page are therefore PCR-conservative for an RAA assay. A fix that runs seqkit at the RAA budget and derives the PCR and RAA metrics separately is prepared; until it is executed on HPC, treat raa_detected_pct as a lower bound and the Module 12 RAA-viable estimate as the biologically appropriate figure.

2. Spatial and Temporal Coverage Aggregation

To identify potential diagnostic gaps, the engine aggregates simulation results into distinct metadata dimensions:

  1. Subtype Coverage: Breaks down detection percentages by viral sub-lineages (e.g., A/H1N1, A/H3N2, A/H5N1) to ensure the assay is truly universal.
  2. Temporal Coverage: Groups detection rates by year of sequence collection (e.g., 2015 to 2025) to track whether modern evolutionary mutations are lowering assay sensitivity over time.
  3. Spatial Coverage: Groups detection rates by country (e.g., Indonesia vs. global) to guarantee high local diagnostic sensitivity.

3. Configuration & Parameterization

The virtual amplification evaluation is parameterized in config.yaml to control mismatch thresholds for both PCR and RAA:

# [M10] In-silico validation parameters (same block as §1.2/§1.3)
insilico_pcr:
  max_mismatch: 3                 # Mismatch budget for the PCR (seqkit) amplification model
  num_candidates: 40             # Candidates measured for coverage per region
  raa_3prime_bp: 4                # Critical 3' anchor zone for RAA (Higgins et al. 2022)
  raa_max_3prime_mm: 1            # Max mismatches allowed in the RAA 3' critical zone
  raa_max_internal_mm: 4          # Max mismatches allowed in the RAA internal body
  raa_probe_max_mm: 5             # Max total probe mismatches (TwistAmp manual 2.7)

3.1. Parameter Tuning Dictionary

Parameter Name Allowed Values Default Value Purpose & Biological Meaning
insilico_pcr.max_mismatch Integer 0 to 5 3 Mismatch budget for the PCR (seqkit) amplification model. NOTE: this currently also gates the RAA metric — see §1.3.
insilico_pcr.raa_3prime_bp Integer 3 to 8 4 Length of the critical 3’ anchor block for RAA. Extension is highly sensitive to mismatch here. The 4-nt value follows Higgins et al. (2022) (the earlier 8 had no source).
insilico_pcr.raa_max_3prime_mm Integer 0 to 2 1 Max mismatches allowed in the 4-bp 3’ critical zone.
insilico_pcr.raa_max_internal_mm Integer 0 to 6 4 Max mismatches in the internal/5’ body. Recombinase-mediated strand invasion tolerates up to 4 internal mismatches.
insilico_pcr.raa_probe_max_mm Integer 0 to 9 5 Max total probe mismatches (probe judged on total load, not 3’ kinetics; TwistAmp 2.7).

3.2. How to Interpret Validation Heatmaps

Module 10 outputs an interactive report (coverage_heatmap.html):

  • PCR vs RAA Amplified: in the current model RAA reads at or below PCR because RAA detection is a subset of the PCR-seqkit -m amplified set (see §1.3) — a modelling artifact, not biology. Under the RAA tolerance itself, RAA coverage is expected to be higher than PCR.
  • Subtype Breakdown Table: Review this table to identify target bias. A primer set with \(99\%\) H3N2 but a low H5N1 figure signals either a real mismatch against a divergent clade OR the PCR-cap artifact (§1.3) — the Module 12 attribution distinguishes the two.

5. Troubleshooting & Limitations

5.1. Low RAA Coverage compared to PCR Coverage

  • Problem: The RAA amplified percentage reads at or below the PCR amplified percentage.
  • Cause: This is a known modelling artifact, not biology (see §1.3). RAA is currently evaluated only on sequences the PCR seqkit -m max_mismatch pass already amplified, so the RAA figure is a subset of PCR and cannot exceed it — the opposite of RAA’s higher mismatch tolerance. For divergent subtypes (e.g. global H5N1) this understates true RAA coverage substantially (31.84% reported vs ~94% RAA-viable).
  • Solution: A fix that runs seqkit at the RAA mismatch budget and derives PCR and RAA separately is prepared (to be run on HPC). Meanwhile use the Module 12 coverage-failure attribution for the RAA-viable estimate, and degenerate base rescue (Module 08) to genuinely raise coverage at variable sites.
