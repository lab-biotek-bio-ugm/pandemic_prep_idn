Module 10: In-silico PCR & RAA Validation

Before biological wet-lab validation, designed primer and probe candidates must undergo virtual simulation to measure their diagnostic sensitivity. Module 10 executes the In-silico Validation Engine (scripts/m10_insilico_pcr.py) using SeqKit (Shen et al. 2016) to run high-throughput binding simulations against large GISAID and NCBI sequence databases.

This step calculates exact diagnostic coverage metrics broken down by subtype, collection year, and country to verify assay robustness against global and local viral diversity.

1. Virtual Amplification and Binding Simulation

The validation engine runs simulated amplifications by matching designed primers and probes against target database sequences using sequence-matching parameters:

1.1. PCR Simulation (seqkit amplicon)

SeqKit’s amplicon command matches primer sequences against every target genome in the database, allowing up to a defined number of mismatches (default: \(3\) or \(4\) mismatches). It returns virtual amplicons for target genomes that match both primers within a designated length range (e.g. 100–300 bp).

1.2. RAA Isothermal Binding Rules

Because RAA operates at \(37^\circ\text{C}\) with a recombinase complex, it is thermodynamically tolerant of minor internal mismatches but highly sensitive to 3’ terminal defects. The engine implements strict, multi-zone mismatch criteria:

  • 3’ Critical Zone: Evaluates the last 8 nucleotides of the 3’ end of both primers. It allows a maximum of 1 mismatch; any more than 1 mismatch in this zone vetoes the binding.
  • Internal Body: Allows a maximum of 4 mismatches across the rest of the 30–35 bp primer body.
  • Probe Binding: Probes (46–52 bp) allow a maximum of 3 mismatches to maintain hybridization-induced exonuclease cleavage.

2. Spatial and Temporal Coverage Aggregation

To identify potential diagnostic gaps, the engine aggregates simulation results into distinct metadata dimensions:

  1. Subtype Coverage: Breaks down detection percentages by viral sub-lineages (e.g., A/H1N1, A/H3N2, A/H5N1) to ensure the assay is truly universal.
  2. Temporal Coverage: Groups detection rates by year of sequence collection (e.g., 2015 to 2025) to track whether modern evolutionary mutations are lowering assay sensitivity over time.
  3. Spatial Coverage: Groups detection rates by country (e.g., Indonesia vs. global) to guarantee high local diagnostic sensitivity.

3. Configuration & Parameterization

The virtual amplification evaluation is parameterized in config.yaml to control mismatch thresholds for both PCR and RAA:

# [M10] In-silico Validation Parameters
validation:
  pcr_mismatch: 2                 # Maximum mismatches allowed for PCR primers (0 to 4)
  raa_3prime_bp: 8                # Length of the critical 3' end region for RAA (bp)
  raa_max_3prime_mm: 1            # Maximum mismatches allowed in the RAA 3' critical zone
  raa_max_internal_mm: 4          # Maximum mismatches allowed in the RAA internal zone

3.1. Parameter Tuning Dictionary

Parameter Name Allowed Values Default Value Purpose & Biological Meaning
validation.pcr_mismatch Integer 0 to 5 2 The maximum mismatches allowed for standard PCR. If a primer has \(\le 2\) mismatches against a sequence, the template is considered amplified. Set this to 0 for high-specificity screening (e.g. strain differentiation) or 3 to model relaxed PCR assays.
validation.raa_3prime_bp Integer 5 to 15 8 The length of the critical 3’ terminal anchoring block of RAA primers. Polymerase extension is highly sensitive to mismatched base pairing at this end.
validation.raa_max_3prime_mm Integer 0 to 2 1 Maximum mismatches allowed in the 8-bp 3’ critical zone. Setting this to 0 represents strict, high-fidelity polymerase constraints.
validation.raa_max_internal_mm Integer 0 to 6 4 Maximum mismatches allowed in the remaining internal/5’ body of the RAA primer. Isothermal recombinase-mediated strand invasion can tolerate up to 4 internal mismatches without aborting.

3.2. How to Interpret Validation Heatmaps

Module 10 outputs an interactive report (coverage_heatmap.html):

  • PCR vs RAA Amplified: RAA coverage is almost always slightly lower than PCR coverage because RAA primers are longer (\(30-35\) bp), which statistically increases the probability of encountering random mutations along the primer body.
  • Subtype Breakdown Table: Review this table to identify target bias. If a primer set has \(99\%\) H3N2 coverage but \(0\%\) H5N1 coverage, it indicates that the primer has a critical mismatch against the avian clade sequence.

5. Troubleshooting & Limitations

5.1. Low RAA Coverage compared to PCR Coverage

  • Problem: The RAA amplified percentage is significantly lower than the PCR amplified percentage.
  • Cause: This occurs in target sets with high sequence variability. RAA primers are longer (30–35 bp) than PCR primers (20 bp), which statistically increases the probability of encountering mismatches along the primer body. In addition, the strict 3’ terminal zone limit (max 1 mismatch in 8 bp) penalizes binding much harder than standard PCR.
  • Solution: Turn on degenerate base rescue in Module 08 to introduce degenerate bases at variable sites, allowing the primer to match multiple variants and restore RAA coverage.
